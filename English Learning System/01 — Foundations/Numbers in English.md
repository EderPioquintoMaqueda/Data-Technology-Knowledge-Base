> Numbers are everywhere in data work: metrics, dates, prices, percentages, versions, phone numbers. Practise reading **every number you see** out loud.

## 2. Tens and the "-teen vs -ty" trap ⚠️
| -teen (stress at the END) | -ty (stress at the START) |
|---|---|
| 13 thir**TEEN** /θɝːˈtiːn/ | 30 **THIR**ty /ˈθɝː.t̬i/ |
| 14 four**TEEN** | 40 **FOR**ty (no "u"!) |
| 15 fif**TEEN** | 50 **FIF**ty |
| 16 six**TEEN** | 60 **SIX**ty |
| 17 seven**TEEN** | 70 **SEV**enty |
| 18 eigh**TEEN** | 80 **EIGH**ty |
| 19 nine**TEEN** | 90 **NINE**ty |
In American English *thirty, forty...* often sound like "**ZER**-di", "**FOR**-di" (flap T). If unsure, ask: *"Is that one-five or five-zero?"*

### Compound numbers 21–99 (use a hyphen)
21 twenty-one · 34 thirty-four · 47 forty-seven · 58 fifty-eight · 99 ninety-nine

## 3. Big numbers
| Number | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | one hundred / a hundred | never "one hundreds" |
| 101 | one hundred **and** one (UK) / one hundred one (US) | |
| 250 | two hundred (and) fifty | |
| 1,000 | one thousand | |
| 1,500 | one thousand five hundred / **fifteen hundred** | informal |
| 10,000 | ten thousand | |
| 100,000 | one hundred thousand | |
| 1,000,000 | one **million** | Spanish *un millón* |
| 1,000,000,000 | one **billion** | ⚠️ = *mil millones* (NOT *un billón*) |
| 10¹² | one **trillion** | = *un billón* en español |

⚠️ **Plural rule:** with a number, *hundred / thousand / million* have **no "s"**: *three million users* ✅ · *three millions users* ❌. With "of" they take "s": *millions of rows* ✅.

### Writing conventions
| English | Spanish (many countries) |
|---|---|
| 1,250,000.75 → comma for thousands, **point** for decimals | 1.250.000,75 |
| 0.5 → "**zero point five**" or "point five" | 0,5 → "cero coma cinco" |

### Reading big numbers
- 2,347 → *two thousand three hundred (and) forty-seven*
- 1,250,300 → *one million two hundred fifty thousand three hundred*
- 7,800,000,000 → *seven point eight billion*
- Data sizes: 1.5M rows → *one point five million rows* · 10K → *ten K / ten thousand*

## 10. Phone numbers, IDs and codes
Say digits one by one, grouped: 55 1234 5678 → *five five, one two three four, five six seven eight*. **0** = *zero* or *oh*. **44** = *four four* or *double four*.
Spelling emails: ana.lopez@mail.com → *ana dot lopez at mail dot com*. `_` underscore · `-` dash/hyphen · `/` slash · `\` backslash.

### Sentences with numbers (read them aloud)
1. *Revenue increased **by 12.5 percent** compared with **the third quarter**.*
2. *The table has **roughly three point two million rows** and **forty-seven columns**.*
3. *The query now runs in **two hundred milliseconds** instead of **four seconds** — that's **twenty times faster**.*
4. *We onboarded **fifteen hundred** new customers **on November eighteenth**.*
5. *Only **one in ten** users completed the funnel, so the conversion rate is **ten percent**.*
