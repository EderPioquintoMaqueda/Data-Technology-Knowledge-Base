# Must and Have To

## 1. Introduction: Obligation and necessity

`Must` y `have to` son estructuras que utilizamos para expresar obligación, necesidad o requisitos.

En español pueden traducirse como:

- Debo...
    
- Tengo que...
    
- Es necesario...
    
- Es obligatorio...
    

Examples:

|English|Spanish|
|---|---|
|I must study.|Debo estudiar.|
|I have to work.|Tengo que trabajar.|
|You must wear a helmet.|Debes usar casco.|
|We have to finish the report.|Tenemos que terminar el informe.|
|Employees must follow the rules.|Los empleados deben seguir las reglas.|

Aunque comparten muchos usos, existen diferencias gramaticales y de intención que debemos comprender.

## 2. MUST: Fundamental characteristics

`Must` es un modal verb. Por lo tanto, sigue las reglas generales de los modales.

### 2.1. Structure

**Subject + must + base verb + complement.**

Examples:

- I must study.
    
- She must work.
    
- He must arrive early.
    
- We must finish the project.
    
- They must follow the instructions.
    

Después de `must`, el verbo principal permanece en su forma base.

|Correct|Incorrect|
|---|---|
|She must work.|She must works.|
|He must study.|He must to study.|
|We must finish.|We must finished.|
|They must leave.|They must leaving.|

### 2.2. No conjugation

`Must` mantiene la misma forma con todos los sujetos.

|Subject|Structure|
|---|---|
|I|must work|
|You|must work|
|He|must work|
|She|must work|
|It|must work|
|We|must work|
|They|must work|

No agregamos `-s` en tercera persona.

### 2.3. Negative structure

La negación se forma agregando `not` después del modal.

**Subject + must not + base verb.**

Examples:

- You must not smoke here.
    
- Employees must not share confidential information.
    
- We must not ignore safety procedures.
    
- The technician must not operate the machine without authorization.
    

La contracción es:

`must not` → `mustn't`

Examples:

- You mustn't enter this area.
    
- We mustn't forget the deadline.
    

**Important:** `Mustn't` expresa prohibición, no ausencia de obligación. Esta diferencia será fundamental más adelante.

### 2.4. Interrogative structure

Para formular preguntas, colocamos `must` antes del sujeto.

**Must + subject + base verb + complement?**

Examples:

- Must I attend the meeting?
    
- Must we finish today?
    
- Must employees wear safety equipment?
    
- Must he submit the report?
    

En conversaciones cotidianas, las preguntas con `must` pueden sonar formales o rígidas. Frecuentemente utilizamos `Do I have to...?` o `Do we have to...?`.

## 3. MUST: Strong obligation

El uso principal de `must` es expresar una obligación fuerte.

Puede comunicar una necesidad que el hablante considera importante o una regla que debe cumplirse.

Examples:

- I must improve my English.
    
- We must finish this project.
    
- You must follow the instructions.
    
- Employees must protect confidential information.
    
- The company must comply with safety regulations.
    

### 3.1. Personal obligation

Utilizamos `must` cuando presentamos una obligación como algo importante desde nuestra perspectiva.

Examples:

- I must study more.
    
- I must organize my schedule.
    
- I must improve my technical skills.
    
- I must prepare for the interview.
    
- We must solve this problem.
    

### 3.2. Strong recommendations

También puede expresar una recomendación muy enfática.

Examples:

- You must read this book.
    
- You must visit that place.
    
- You must try this application.
    
- You must learn how to use SQL.
    

En estos casos no siempre existe una obligación legal. El hablante enfatiza que considera algo muy importante o recomendable.

### 3.3. Rules and instructions

`Must` es frecuente en reglamentos, instrucciones y políticas.

Examples:

- Visitors must register at reception.
    
- Employees must wear identification badges.
    
- Users must create a secure password.
    
- All documents must include the required information.
    

En este contexto, la obligación puede ser institucional y no solamente personal.

## 4. HAVE TO: Obligation and necessity

`Have to` también expresa obligación y necesidad, pero gramaticalmente funciona de manera diferente a `must`.

Aunque su significado es parecido, `have to` utiliza el verbo `have` como parte de una estructura que cambia según el tiempo y el sujeto.

### 4.1. Present structure

**Subject + have/has to + base verb + complement.**

|Subject|Structure|
|---|---|
|I|have to work|
|You|have to work|
|He|has to work|
|She|has to work|
|It|has to work|
|We|have to work|
|They|have to work|

Observa que utilizamos `has to` con he, she e it.

Examples:

- I have to study.
    
- She has to work tomorrow.
    
- He has to submit the report.
    
- We have to attend the meeting.
    
- They have to follow the procedure.
    

### 4.2. External obligation

`Have to` se utiliza frecuentemente cuando la obligación proviene de circunstancias externas, reglas, requisitos o decisiones institucionales.

Examples:

- I have to arrive at the office at nine.
    
- Employees have to follow company policies.
    
- We have to submit the documents before Friday.
    
- The technician has to inspect the equipment.
    
- Students have to complete the required courses.
    

### 4.3. Everyday necessity

También es muy común para expresar necesidades cotidianas.

Examples:

- I have to wake up early.
    
- She has to buy groceries.
    
- We have to clean the house.
    
- He has to take the bus.
    
- They have to pay the electricity bill.
    

### 4.4. Professional requirements

Examples:

- I have to prepare monthly reports.
    
- We have to maintain accurate records.
    
- The team has to review the documentation.
    
- Engineers have to follow safety procedures.
    
- The company has to protect customer information.
    

## 5. MUST vs. HAVE TO

Ambas estructuras pueden expresar obligación y en muchos contextos son intercambiables.

Sin embargo, existe una diferencia tradicional de perspectiva.

- `Must`: presenta la obligación como algo que el hablante considera necesario o enfatiza.
    
- `Have to`: suele presentar la obligación como un requisito, una regla o una circunstancia externa.
    

Compare:

- I must improve my English.
    
- I have to improve my English.
    

La primera puede transmitir una decisión o necesidad personal. La segunda puede presentar una necesidad derivada de las circunstancias, por ejemplo, porque el trabajo exige inglés.

### 5.1. Comparison table

|Situation|Must|Have to|
|---|---|---|
|Personal obligation|Very common|Very common|
|External requirement|Common|Very common|
|Strong recommendation|Very common|Possible|
|Institutional rules|Very common|Very common|
|Everyday obligations|Common|Very common|
|Formal written instructions|Very common|Common|

No debemos convertir esta diferencia en una regla absoluta. En el inglés moderno, los hablantes utilizan ambas estructuras para expresar obligaciones, y el contexto determina el matiz.

## 6. Negative forms: Mustn't vs. Don't have to

Esta es una de las diferencias más importantes de todo el sistema de modal verbs.

`Mustn't` significa que algo está prohibido.

`Don't have to` significa que algo no es necesario.

No expresan lo mismo.

### 6.1. Mustn't: Prohibition

Structure:

**Subject + must not + base verb.**

Examples:

- You mustn't smoke here.
    
- You mustn't enter this area.
    
- Employees mustn't share passwords.
    
- Visitors mustn't touch the equipment.
    
- We mustn't ignore safety regulations.
    

Traducción:

- No debes fumar aquí.
    
- Está prohibido entrar en esta área.
    
- Los empleados no deben compartir contraseñas.
    
- Los visitantes no deben tocar el equipo.
    

La acción no está permitida.

### 6.2. Don't have to: Lack of necessity

Structure:

**Subject + do/does + not + have to + base verb.**

Examples:

- I don't have to work tomorrow.
    
- She doesn't have to attend the meeting.
    
- We don't have to finish today.
    
- They don't have to bring their computers.
    

Significa que la acción no es obligatoria. La persona puede realizarla si quiere, pero no está obligada.

### 6.3. Comparison

|Sentence|Meaning|
|---|---|
|You mustn't leave.|Está prohibido que te vayas.|
|You don't have to leave.|No es necesario que te vayas.|
|You mustn't use your phone.|No está permitido utilizar el teléfono.|
|You don't have to use your phone.|No necesitas utilizar el teléfono.|
|We mustn't finish today.|No debemos terminar hoy / se nos indica que no lo hagamos.|
|We don't have to finish today.|No es necesario terminar hoy.|

**Essential rule:**

- `Mustn't` = prohibition.
    
- `Don't have to` = no obligation.
    

### 6.4. Must not vs. Do not have to in professional contexts

Consider these examples:

- Employees must not access confidential files without authorization.
    
- Employees do not have to work on weekends.
    

La primera oración establece una restricción.

La segunda elimina una obligación.

Esta distinción es esencial para comprender reglamentos, contratos, procedimientos y políticas empresariales.

## 7. HAVE TO: Negative and interrogative forms

A diferencia de `must`, `have to` funciona como una estructura que utiliza los auxiliares `do` y `does` en Present Simple.

### 7.1. Negative structure

**Subject + do/does + not + have to + base verb.**

|Subject|Negative|
|---|---|
|I|don't have to|
|You|don't have to|
|He|doesn't have to|
|She|doesn't have to|
|It|doesn't have to|
|We|don't have to|
|They|don't have to|

Examples:

- I don't have to work today.
    
- She doesn't have to drive.
    
- He doesn't have to attend the meeting.
    
- We don't have to pay now.
    
- They don't have to submit another document.
    

Observa que después de `doesn't` utilizamos `have`, nunca `has`.

Incorrect: She doesn't has to work.

Correct: She doesn't have to work.

### 7.2. Interrogative structure

**Do/Does + subject + have to + base verb + complement?**

Examples:

- Do you have to work tomorrow?
    
- Does she have to attend the meeting?
    
- Do we have to submit the report?
    
- Does he have to wear a uniform?
    
- Do they have to register?
    

### 7.3. Short answers

|Question|Affirmative|Negative|
|---|---|---|
|Do you have to work?|Yes, I do.|No, I don't.|
|Does she have to study?|Yes, she does.|No, she doesn't.|
|Do they have to attend?|Yes, they do.|No, they don't.|

No repetimos toda la estructura en una respuesta corta.

### 7.4. WH-questions

Examples:

- Why do you have to leave?
    
- When does she have to arrive?
    
- Where do we have to register?
    
- What do they have to prepare?
    
- How much do I have to pay?
    

En contextos profesionales:

- When do we have to submit the report?
    
- Which documents does the company have to provide?
    
- Why do employees have to follow this procedure?
    

## 8. MUST: Questions and short answers

`Must` forma preguntas sin utilizar `do` o `does`.

Examples:

- Must I sign this document?
    
- Must we finish the project today?
    
- Must employees wear protective equipment?
    

Short answers:

- Yes, you must.
    
- No, you mustn't.
    

Sin embargo, en conversaciones cotidianas, las preguntas con `must` pueden sonar poco naturales o demasiado formales.

Frecuentemente preferimos:

- Do I have to sign this document?
    
- Do we have to finish today?
    
- Does everyone have to attend?
    

Estas estructuras son más comunes en el inglés conversacional.

## 9. HAVE TO in different tenses

Una ventaja importante de `have to` es que puede utilizarse en diferentes tiempos verbales.

`Must` no tiene todas las formas verbales que necesitamos para expresar obligaciones en el pasado o el futuro.

### 9.1. Present obligation

- I have to work.
    
- She has to study.
    
- We have to finish the project.
    

### 9.2. Past obligation: Had to

Para expresar una obligación pasada utilizamos `had to`.

Structure:

**Subject + had to + base verb.**

Examples:

- I had to work yesterday.
    
- She had to attend the meeting.
    
- We had to repair the machine.
    
- They had to submit the documents last week.
    

La estructura `had to` funciona como Past Simple.

Negative:

**Subject + did not + have to + base verb.**

Examples:

- I didn't have to work yesterday.
    
- She didn't have to attend the meeting.
    
- We didn't have to repair the equipment.
    

Interrogative:

**Did + subject + have to + base verb?**

Examples:

- Did you have to work yesterday?
    
- Did she have to sign the document?
    
- Did they have to pay for the service?
    

Importante: después de `did` utilizamos `have`, no `had`.

### 9.3. Future obligation: Will have to

Para expresar una obligación futura utilizamos `will have to`.

Structure:

**Subject + will have to + base verb.**

Examples:

- I will have to study tomorrow.
    
- She will have to travel next week.
    
- We will have to update the system.
    
- They will have to complete the project.
    

Negative:

- I won't have to work tomorrow.
    
- She won't have to attend the meeting.
    

Interrogative:

- Will you have to travel?
    
- Will we have to update the database?
    

### 9.4. Present Perfect obligation: Have had to

También podemos expresar obligaciones que han existido durante un periodo relacionado con el presente.

Structure:

**Subject + have/has + had to + base verb.**

Examples:

- I have had to work long hours.
    
- She has had to learn new technologies.
    
- We have had to solve several technical problems.
    
- They have had to change the procedure.
    

La primera forma de `have/has` funciona como auxiliar del Present Perfect. La segunda palabra `had` forma parte de la estructura de obligación.

### 9.5. Comparison table

|Time|Structure|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Present|Have/has to|I have to work.|
|Past|Had to|I had to work.|
|Future|Will have to|I will have to work.|
|Present Perfect|Have/has had to|I have had to work.|

## 10. MUST: Present and future obligation

`Must` puede utilizarse para expresar obligaciones presentes y futuras.

Examples:

- I must finish this report.
    
- We must improve the process.
    
- You must submit the application tomorrow.
    
- Employees must attend the training next Monday.
    

Aunque `must` puede referirse a una obligación futura, no es la forma habitual para expresar todas las obligaciones futuras.

Cuando queremos hablar de un requisito futuro de manera explícita, `will have to` suele ser una alternativa muy útil.

Compare:

- You must submit the report tomorrow.
    
- You will have to submit the report tomorrow.
    

Ambas expresan una obligación futura. La segunda presenta el requisito desde una perspectiva futura.

## 11. MUST: Strong deduction

Además de obligación, `must` puede expresar una conclusión lógica basada en evidencia.

En este uso no significa «deber», sino «debe de ser», «seguramente» o «tiene que ser».

Examples:

- She must be tired.
    
- He must be at work.
    
- They must know the answer.
    
- The machine must be overheating.
    
- There must be an error in the database.
    

### 11.1. Logical deduction

Utilizamos `must` cuando tenemos información que nos lleva a considerar algo muy probable.

Example:

The machine is making a strange noise and its temperature is increasing.

Conclusion:

- The machine must have a mechanical problem.
    

No estamos estableciendo una obligación para la máquina. Estamos expresando una conclusión basada en evidencia.

### 11.2. Must vs. May and Might

Compare:

- The server must be offline. (La evidencia apunta fuertemente a que está desconectado).
    
- The server may be offline. (Es posible que esté desconectado).
    
- The server might be offline. (Podría estar desconectado; posibilidad tentativa).
    

Esta diferencia es importante al comunicar diagnósticos técnicos, hipótesis y resultados.

## 12. MUST HAVE + Past Participle: Past deduction

También podemos utilizar `must have + past participle` para expresar una conclusión lógica sobre un acontecimiento pasado.

Structure:

**Subject + must + have + past participle.**

Examples:

- She must have forgotten the meeting.
    
- He must have left early.
    
- The server must have stopped working.
    
- The technician must have changed the configuration.
    
- They must have received the wrong information.
    

La estructura indica que el hablante considera muy probable que algo haya sucedido.

Compare:

- She must be tired. (Deducción presente).
    
- She must have been tired. (Deducción sobre un estado pasado).
    
- The system must be damaged. (Deducción presente).
    
- The system must have been damaged. (Deducción sobre el pasado).
    

## 13. MUST vs. HAVE TO vs. SHOULD

Aunque las tres estructuras pueden relacionarse con responsabilidades, sus significados no son iguales.

### 13.1. Must

Expresa obligación fuerte, necesidad o deducción lógica.

- You must wear a helmet.
    
- I must finish this project.
    
- He must be tired.
    

### 13.2. Have to

Expresa obligación o necesidad, frecuentemente relacionada con requisitos externos.

- I have to work tomorrow.
    
- Employees have to follow the procedure.
    
- We had to change the schedule.
    

### 13.3. Should

Expresa consejo, recomendación o expectativa.

- You should study more.
    
- We should review the report.
    
- The company should improve its processes.
    

### 13.4. Comparison table

|Modal|Main meaning|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Must|Strong obligation|You must wear safety equipment.|
|Have to|Requirement or necessity|I have to attend the meeting.|
|Should|Advice or recommendation|You should review the document.|

**Important:** `Should` se estudiará con mayor profundidad en el siguiente documento correspondiente a recomendaciones y consejos.

## 14. Professional English: Technology, Engineering and Business

### 14.1. Technology and software

- Developers must protect sensitive information.
    
- We have to update the application.
    
- Users must create secure passwords.
    
- The team has to review the code.
    
- We had to restore the database.
    
- The company will have to improve its infrastructure.
    

### 14.2. Data analysis

- We must verify the quality of the data.
    
- Analysts have to identify missing values.
    
- The team must document the methodology.
    
- We had to clean the dataset before analyzing it.
    
- The company will have to improve its reporting process.
    

### 14.3. Engineering and machinery

- Operators must wear protective equipment.
    
- Technicians have to inspect the machinery.
    
- We must follow the maintenance schedule.
    
- The machine must not operate without authorization.
    
- We had to replace the damaged component.
    
- The equipment will have to undergo maintenance.
    

### 14.4. Office and business communication

- I have to prepare the monthly report.
    
- We must meet the deadline.
    
- Employees have to follow company policies.
    
- You must not share confidential information.
    
- We don't have to attend the meeting tomorrow.
    
- The department will have to review the budget.

## 17. Integrated narrative

### Workplace responsibilities

**English**

When I started working in the department, I had to learn several administrative and technical procedures.

I have to prepare reports, review documentation and maintain accurate records. I also have to coordinate information with different teams.

Employees must follow institutional policies and protect confidential information. They must not share sensitive documents without authorization.

Sometimes, we have to solve unexpected problems. When a machine requires maintenance, the technical team must inspect the equipment before it can operate again.

Last month, we had to review several records because some information was incomplete.

In the future, we will have to improve the reporting process and automate certain tasks.

I must also continue improving my English because it is an important skill for my professional development.

**Español**

Cuando comencé a trabajar en el departamento, tuve que aprender varios procedimientos administrativos y técnicos.

Tengo que preparar reportes, revisar documentación y mantener registros precisos. También tengo que coordinar información con diferentes equipos.

Los empleados deben seguir las políticas institucionales y proteger la información confidencial. No deben compartir documentos sensibles sin autorización.

En ocasiones, tenemos que resolver problemas inesperados. Cuando una máquina requiere mantenimiento, el equipo técnico debe inspeccionar el equipo antes de que pueda volver a operar.

El mes pasado tuvimos que revisar varios registros porque parte de la información estaba incompleta.

En el futuro tendremos que mejorar el proceso de elaboración de reportes y automatizar ciertas tareas.

También debo continuar mejorando mi inglés porque es una habilidad importante para mi desarrollo profesional.

## 20. Essential rules to remember

1. `Must` es un modal verb y no cambia según el sujeto.
    
2. `Have to` utiliza `have` o `has` en presente.
    
3. Después de `must` y `have to` utilizamos el verbo en su forma base.
    
4. `Must` y `have to` pueden expresar obligaciones similares.
    
5. `Mustn't` expresa prohibición.
    
6. `Don't have to` expresa ausencia de obligación.
    
7. Para expresar obligaciones pasadas utilizamos `had to`.
    
8. Para expresar obligaciones futuras utilizamos `will have to`.
    
9. Para formular preguntas con `have to` utilizamos `do` o `does` en presente.
    
10. Para expresar una deducción lógica podemos utilizar `must + base verb`.
    
11. Para expresar una deducción sobre el pasado utilizamos `must have + past participle`.
    
12. `Should` expresa recomendaciones y no tiene exactamente el mismo significado que `must`.
    

---

## Final learning principle

La diferencia más importante que debemos conservar es:

- **MUST:** debo hacerlo; expreso una obligación fuerte o una conclusión lógica.
    
- **HAVE TO:** tengo que hacerlo; existe una obligación o necesidad.
    
- **MUSTN'T:** está prohibido hacerlo.
    
- **DON'T HAVE TO:** no es necesario hacerlo.
    
- **HAD TO:** tuve que hacerlo.
    
- **WILL HAVE TO:** tendré que hacerlo.
    

Comprender estas diferencias permitirá interpretar correctamente instrucciones, políticas empresariales, procedimientos técnicos y conversaciones cotidianas.
